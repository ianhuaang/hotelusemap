Subject: Regulatory review — what we changed, and what we found


Thank you for the audit. Pulling certificates of occupancy on individual
buildings found things our aggregates were hiding, and it changed the tool
more than the last two rounds of feedback combined. It also turned up four
defects we were not looking for, listed at the bottom.

Everything below is on a branch and not yet live. The one item that needs a
decision from you is at the end.


YOUR EIGHT FINDINGS

1. Union
  Done, and differently than we planned. Rather than infer, the tool now
  reads the Hotel Trades Council's own roster — 377 shops, every one
  geocoded, matched to buildings by position rather than by name.

  237 match buildings we hold. 38 of those are union-covered and no longer
  hotels, which is the finding: HTC keeps representing the workers after a
  conversion, so the contract outlives the hotel. Their shopType field says
  what the building became — Residence, Club, Shelter.

  18 of those 38 sit in segments the tool was calling available capacity.
  569 and 525 Lexington, Bretton Hall, the Downtown Club.

  Your licence-term idea we tested rather than shipped. The roster settles
  union status for 493 licensed buildings:

      3-year term    89 union of 210    42.4%
      1-year term    92 union of 283    32.5%
      base rate                         36.7%

  Statistically real, practically useless — wrong 58% of the time it fires,
  misses half of all union buildings, five points of lift over guessing. We
  took the flag out. Worth knowing the correlation exists and does not mean
  what it looks like.

2. Supportive housing
  Done, via the current-use check described under 4. Catches Project Renewal,
  Praxis, Palladia, HDFCs and the Longacre and Varet Street shelters,
  including ones run by non-profits with no tell in the ownership record.

3. NPISA and Use Group
  Open, and the only real project left. Use group is not in any dataset we
  pull — it lives inside the certificate of occupancy PDFs. A parser was
  scaffolded and abandoned. See the open question.

4. Student housing
  Done. The root cause runs under findings 2, 3 and 4 and the Watchtower
  scenario: every city source answers what a building is permitted to be,
  never what it is.

  569 Lexington is the case. 730 HPD Class B units, 100% transient, tiered
  as legal transient at medium confidence. C of O, HPD registration and
  building class all agree with each other and all miss that it is a
  dormitory.

  The tool now asks Google what is at the building's coordinates. 2,066 of
  2,615 buildings answered; 124 contradict their tier. 5,289 registered
  Class B rooms sit in buildings that are not transient uses — 5.7% of all
  Class B capacity we checked. The large ones:

      730  569 Lexington Avenue    FOUND Study Midtown East
      506  525 Lexington Avenue    FOUND Study Turtle Bay
      492  99 Washington Street    FOUND Study Financial District
      492  500 Riverside Drive     International House
      478  106 East 14 Street      NYU University Hall
      253  345 West 86 Street      Queen of the World's Residence
      222  55 John Street          EHS 55 John Street Residence
      204  129 Madison Avenue      Madison Avenue Baptist Church
      168  317 West 45 Street      Longacre Shelter

  Caveat worth stating: 534 buildings, 21%, returned no verifiable occupant.
  For a residential block with no registered business that is an answer
  rather than a gap, but a fifth of the map has no signal either way.

5. Condos
  Done, and you were right that DOB is unreliable — we do not use it. We were
  reading the owner name for the string "CONDO", which matched SECONDO and
  CONDOR REALTY LLC, plus a class list covering three of the fifteen R codes.

  Finance gives every condominium unit a billing lot numbered 7501 or above.
  Across the full 15,176-building pipeline: 1,118 condos before, 3,585 now,
  of which 1,814 reach the map. Including 1335 Avenue of the Americas and
  1535 Broadway — the two largest Class B counts we hold were outside the
  filter entirely.

6 & 7. Safe Hotels thresholds
  Done, and the counting question mattered more than the thresholds. The Act
  counts guest rooms — rooms used for transient occupancy, explicitly
  excluding residential and SRO units. So Class B is the measure and total
  units is not:

      66 Pine Street     165 Class B, 612 Class A   total would say 777
      116 John Street    183 Class B, 233 Class A   total would say 416

  116 John crosses 400 on total units and picks up continuous security
  obligations. It is a 183-room hotel. Of the 4,469 buildings in mapped
  tiers, 458 cross 100 rooms and 80 cross 400.
  Where the only figure is an estimate the flag stays silent, because a guess
  should not assert a legal threshold.

8. Facade / Local Law 11
  Done as a flag — 2,748 of 4,469 mapped buildings are over six storeys. What
  the last inspection cycle found lives in DOB facade filings, which we do not
  pull, so the flag says the programme applies and no more.


YOUR RECOMMENDATIONS

  Safe Hotels licence export — you were right, and it was a live defect. A
  DCWP licence alone was marking a building as having an active operator,
  which segmented it away from the available list. Since May 2025 the licensee
  is the owner, who may run the hotel, contract it out, or hold a licence on a
  building nobody operates. The fields are now distinct.

  Zoning use group via ZoLa — see finding 3.

  DOB violations for illegal hotels — added, and it needed no new source. We
  were already holding 215,181 ECB rows and pulling only a coarse type field.
  The statute is §28-210.3, permanent dwelling offered or used for other than
  permanent residential purpose. It reads zero until the next refresh re-pulls
  with the added columns.

  97 Columbia Heights — your open question, answered without needing a
  religious-ownership rule, which would have been a blunt instrument. The
  building is no longer Watchtower's dormitory. It reads as FOUND Study
  Columbia Heights Waterfront, student housing, 105 Class B rooms.

  Updated-as-of timestamp — already shipped. Both maps show last refresh and
  every record carries its pull date.

  Hotel Owners of New York v. DCWP — noted, not a code item. We will watch it.

  Pre-December 9 2021 permitted use — implemented, and it cut hard. Reversion
  candidates were asserted from building class and unit mix before any C of O
  history was attached. They are now tested against the cutoff where the
  certificates actually are: 27 candidates, 5 with evidence of transient use
  before 2021-12-09, 22 flagged for review rather than deleted. A gap in the
  record is not proof the grandfathering is gone.

  Prohibited buildings list — not done. We have not confirmed it is published
  in a machine-readable form and would rather not promise a date until we have.


FOUR THINGS YOU DID NOT ASK ABOUT

  The two certificate feeds disagree on date format — BIS writes 2012-10-09,
  DOB NOW writes 11/12/25. Sorted as plain strings, every legacy date outranks
  every NOW date. 727 buildings were displaying a latest C of O that was not
  the latest. One reversion candidate showed 2012 while holding certificates
  from 2025 and 2026.

  Sixteen private membership clubs — the New York Athletic Club at 188 rooms,
  the University Club at 104, the Century Association, the Metropolitan, the
  Colony, the Yacht Club, Knickerbocker, Lotos — register Class B rooms and
  tier as transient targets. Every one of them is an HTC union shop. Member
  sleeping rooms are transient capacity on paper and unbuyable in practice.
  You did not name this category; the data did.

  The panel labelled every DCWP licence "Active" regardless of status.
  Surrendered, Voided and Suspended all displayed as active.

  Some hotel names are attached to the wrong building. "60 Pine Street"
  displays as "Mint House 70 Pine by Kasa" — a real hotel down the block. The
  old lookup never confirmed the address matched. Not yet fixed, and you would
  find it by sampling, so we are telling you before you do.


ONE OPEN QUESTION

  Is use group worth building?

  It is the last item from your review that is a project rather than a task,
  and it means parsing certificate of occupancy PDFs. Before we spend that,
  we want your read: how much of what you wanted use group for is already
  answered by knowing what is in the building today?

  Our instinct is that current use covers the operational question — is this
  a dorm, a shelter, an NPISA — while use group covers the entitlement
  question, which matters when a special permit is in play. If that is right,
  use group is worth building for the ULURP-sensitive subset rather than for
  all 15,000 buildings. If it is wrong, we would rather know now.

  Separately, and for counsel rather than for you: how guest rooms are counted
  where the hotel is only part of a building. The Act says "part of such
  building", and our split-use stock is where the 100 and 400 thresholds
  actually bite.
