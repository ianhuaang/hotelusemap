--- 5. CONDOS -------------------------------------------------------------
> This data is on DOB but may not always be super accurate

We do not use DOB for this. We take it from PLUTO, on two fields that agree
with each other: the Finance tax lot, where every condominium unit is billed
on a lot numbered 7501 or above, and the building class, where the R family is
the condominium family.

Across 15,176 buildings the two disagree once — 234 West 42nd Street, on a
condo lot with an H1 hotel class. That is why we trust it over DOB.

Our own method before this was worse than DOB: 1,118 condos found, against
3,585 now, 1,814 of them on the map.

What the tool does with it: every condo carries a note on the building that it
needs board approval or a commercial condo owner negotiation, and there is a
filter to hide them.

That filter used to be on by default, back when it was catching 386 buildings.
It now catches 1,814, so we turned it off — leaving it on meant opening the
tool to 31% of its own data, with 28,343 Class B rooms behind a checkbox. 1535
Broadway, 1335 Avenue of the Americas and 569 Lexington are all held in condo
form. Better to show the building and the difficulty together.

--- R8. PROHIBITED BUILDINGS LIST -----------------------------------------
> it may be smart to cross-reference with this list of prohibited buildings

Checked, and the list is not published. The OSE page is the application form
owners use to add or remove a building — no list on it, nothing downloadable,
and nothing on Open Data.

OSE does publish the STR Registration Dataset, which is the inverse: buildings
with an active short-term rental registration, with BIN, so it would join
straight to us. Say the word and we will add it.

Worth noting for the cross-reference you had in mind, OSE state that
"inclusion on or absence from the prohibited building list does not alter the
legal occupancy or zoning use group of a building". So it reads as owner
intent rather than a legal bar, which is how you framed it.

To get the list itself we think it is a FOIL request or a direct ask to OSE.
If you have a route in, that is faster than ours.

--- TWO THINGS BACK TO YOU ------------------------------------------------

Is use group worth building?

Our read: current use covers the operational question — is this a dorm, a
shelter, an NPISA — and that is done. Use group covers entitlement, which
matters for a smaller set: buildings where a use change is contemplated, and
manufacturing districts where Use Group 3 sits on a density bonus.

If that is right, we would parse certificates at diligence for specific
buildings rather than build a citywide parser. If you would not trust a
building without its use group on file, say so. That is a different project.

A costed improvement we have not made.

Part of that 21% no-answer rate is our own test being too strict. It throws
out anything filed on a cross street — the Wythe Hotel occupies 75 North 11th
Street and registers as 80 Wythe Avenue.

We have built a better test and not run it. Doing so costs about $85 in API
calls. Flagging it so you know the 21% is partly ours, not all real.
