--- 6 & 7. SAFE HOTELS ROOM THRESHOLDS -------------------------------------
> You may want to understand if directly employing core employees makes a
> difference to underwriting
> Another factor in deciding whether operating a hotel of this size makes
> sense for Kasa

Both flagged. The counting question mattered more than the thresholds. The Act
counts guest rooms — rooms used for transient occupancy, explicitly excluding
residential and SRO units — so Class B is the measure and total units is not:

    66 Pine Street     165 Class B, 612 Class A   total would say 777
    116 John Street    183 Class B, 233 Class A   total would say 416

116 John crosses 400 on total units and picks up continuous security
obligations. It is a 183-room hotel.

We also had Class B wrong in the other direction. Class B is a Multiple
Dwelling Law category covering hotels, rooming houses and SRO alike, and the
Act excludes SRO. 35 buildings were crossing 100 rooms on dormitory and SRO
stock, International House at 492 and NYU University Hall at 478 among them.
Neither is a hotel, so neither carries the duty. Those classes are excluded
now: 372 buildings cross 100 rooms and 67 cross 400.

--- TWO THINGS BACK TO YOU ------------------------------------------------

Is use group worth building?

Our read: current use answers the operational question — is this a dorm, a
shelter, an NPISA, is somebody already running it — and that is now covered.
Use group answers the entitlement question, which matters for a subset:
buildings where a use change is contemplated, and manufacturing districts
where Use Group 3 sits on a density bonus.

If that is right, we would parse certificates at diligence for specific
buildings rather than build a citywide parser. If you would not trust a
building without its use group on file, say so. That is a different project.

A costed improvement we have not spent on.

Part of that 21% no-signal rate is our own test being strict: we accept a
business as belonging to a building by matching the house number it registers,
which throws out anything filed on a cross street. The Wythe Hotel occupies 75
North 11th Street and registers as 80 Wythe Avenue.

The better test is whether the business stands inside the building's
footprint, which we already hold. It is built, and on the cases we checked by
hand it accepts the Wythe and still rejects the building next door. Applying
it means re-running 2,600 lookups, about $85 beyond this month's free
allowance. We have not spent it, and are flagging it as a known, costed
improvement rather than an unknown.
